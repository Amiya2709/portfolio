01 · Pharmaceutical · Alcon

Regulated eye care that still sounds human

Pataday, Systane, Opti-Free, Clear Care and Precision1 contact lenses: always-on UGC and eye-doctor ads, every one approved before it ran.

UGC mashupsEye-doctor adsMAP approvalsVeeva codesCreator consent

The brief

A steady monthly flow of ads across four eye-care lines, where every claim needs medical, legal and regulatory sign-off and every creator needs signed consent.

What I did

  • Rewrote each creator's own story into a compliant script, so approved claims sat inside natural lines.
  • Made a standalone verbal disclosure a default ask for every Alcon creator, so it can be added or cut without a reshoot.
  • Built ad trackers linked to Veeva code registries and consent records for each brand.

What changed

  • One-off legal feedback became standing rules, so the same rejection didn't come back twice.
  • Status updates for Clear Care, Systane and Opti-Free went from hours of copying to seconds.
  • The tracker pattern built for one brand rolled out to the others.

02 · Automotive · Cox Automotive & Tire Agent

Casting the doubt instead of arguing with it

Autotrader and Kelley Blue Book, plus Tire Agent's always-on ad sets, including Spanish-language mashups.

Casting anglesHook testingMashup ad setsWeekly client syncs

The brief

Promote buying a car fully online, a decision people hesitate over, and keep a tyre brand fed with fresh ads every month from a limited pool of footage.

What I did

  • Wrote 10 buyer personas and 11 creative angles beyond the brief, like the "I closed the tab three times" confession.
  • Developed the Autotrader brief through six rounds of client feedback.
  • Built mashup sets with a fixed body and CTA, so swapping the hook made every set a clean test.

What changed

  • A persona library the team could reuse for later briefs.
  • Steady monthly delivery from a limited footage pool.
  • Brand editing guidelines kept different editors cutting the same way.

03 · Retail, food & services

Seasonal moments, shipped on the date that matters

Build-A-Bear, Vanity Fair, Capriotti's and El Car Wash: gifting, holidays, menu launches and before-and-after transformations.

Seasonal campaignsPDP versionsCreator notesIn-store shoots

The brief

High monthly volume (up to 18 Build-A-Bear ads a month) with every holiday and launch landing on time and on brand.

What I did

  • When licensed toys showed up in Build-A-Bear shots, I traced it to a missing "what to avoid" list, owned the miss, and proposed a banned-brand list for every retail shoot.
  • Reworked a Halloween angle into "phone time vs Halloween prep", with the phone out of frame by the end.
  • Sent creators warm, specific notes listing only the two to four missing pieces.

What changed

  • A repeatable guard against off-brand products for any retail shoot.
  • Quick fixes with creator goodwill intact.
  • Client reviews got easier with one-line angles marked as internally approved.

04 · Wellness & fitness

Claims kept careful, energy kept high

Botanic Choice supplements and beauty, In-Shape and Fitness SF gyms.

Supplement claimsDon'ts listsContent rescuesCreator sourcing

The brief

Monthly supplement and gym ads that feel motivating while staying inside what each product can claim.

What I did

  • Added a Don'ts list to the Botanic Choice joint-comfort brief after content said "it takes away the pain".
  • When a creator filmed strength training for a recovery concept, asked only for recovery-amenity clips paired with existing audio.
  • Flagged when the creator pool showed the same faces, as a cue to source new ones.

What changed

  • Briefs that close the gaps recent content exposed.
  • Footage saved without a new voiceover or full reshoot.
  • Fresher variety in the next round of creators.
